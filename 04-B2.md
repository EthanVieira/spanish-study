# Level B2: Vantage

**Prerequisite:** Milestone B1 reached (step B1-627 ticked). The README methods still apply, and the B1 day continues in the heavier B2 form defined below.

**Goal at exit:** you can follow extended speech, television news and current-affairs debate, films in standard Spanish of any major variety and most natural conversation between native speakers; read contemporary literary prose, newspaper opinion pieces and official documents with ease; take part in discussion with fluency, argue a position, defend it under questioning and use the discourse markers and fillers that make speech sound Spanish; and write clear, well-structured texts of 300 to 500 words in the right register, including a formal complaint, a review, a summary and an *artículo de opinión*. Vocabulary about 8000 words. Grammar is complete and mostly automatic, including the whole subjunctive system; remaining work is register, nuance and error repair. This is the level of the DELE B2 and of most Spanish-speaking workplaces.

**Time:** the dailies take about 2 hours a day (half of it the commute slot and free reading, which need no desk) and the steps add up to about 580 hours: about 1030 hours in all. At the planned pace, over 34 weeks, that is 2 hours 50 minutes of steps a day, 6 days a week; at one hour of steps a day the level takes about 97 weeks. Keep the order either way.

**What changes at B2** (Appendix A): everything is native material. Grammar is studied in Spanish first (*Uso de la gramática española: avanzado* and the RAE's *Nueva gramática básica*) and then in depth in English (Butt and Benjamin). *La casa de papel* and Netflix's *Cien años de soledad* replace *El ministerio del tiempo*. Literature is the centre: Laforet, García Márquez, Pérez-Reverte, Borges, Mariana Enríquez, Rulfo, Delibes, Sara Mesa, Neruda and Cortázar. Non-fiction is a popular history of Spain read alongside RTVE's *Memoria de España*, plus a daily newspaper and two long-form magazines. Speaking adds debate and news retelling. Writing reaches 300 to 500 words and the opinion column. Underneath, the dailies from B1 continue in B2 form: the commute slot carries *Hoy en EL PAÍS* and the audiobook of the book you are reading, and free reading moves to full-length popular novels. Every week has two series steps (*Isabel* into season 3, and a Mexican series beside *Cien años de soledad*). Films go from ten to sixteen.

## The B2 day

Every study day: **the dailies, in the order of the table, then the current step**, the first unticked step in this file. One step is one sitting with one focus; a step too long for today is resumed tomorrow. The old weekly rhythm and the weekly habits are now individual steps placed in order inside each week: nothing runs in the background except the dailies.

| Daily | Minutes | Starts at | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anki: all due cards, then 20 new (half of them sentence cards) | 20 | A1-004 | never |
| Commute slot: today's *Hoy en EL PAÍS* with a three-sentence note, then the audiobook of the current book | 30 | B2-007 | C1 changes its content |
| One newspaper article, source by the three-day cycle, with a two-sentence summary and one harvested card | 15 | B2-010 | C1 raises it to two |
| AI voice conversation, topic fixed by the rule in B2-009 | 15 | B2-009 | never |
| The daily song, sung twice | 5 | A1 | never |
| Diario, 10 sentences (every second entry a 100-word reflection from Week 10), corrected on Sundays | 15 | B2-011 | end of B2 |
| Free reading, from the fixed list | 25 | B2-008 | never |

A week check passes when every step since the previous check is ticked and the dailies were done on at least six of the last seven days.

The steps that return every week are written as one line and use these patterns:

- **Long-form pattern**: the episode named, extensive first; then a written 8-sentence summary in Spanish; then a second listen of the 5 minutes you understood least, pausing to write down every phrase you can catch.
- **Telediario pattern**: the day's summary edition on the RTVE Noticias YouTube channel, no captions, no pausing; the five headlines in Spanish, checked on rtve.es/noticias.
- **Retelling**: straight after the commute slot's *Hoy en EL PAÍS*, a 3-minute recorded retelling of its argument, without notes.
- **Monologue**: 5 minutes on the topic named (Appendix F part 2), recorded, transcribed and corrected.
- **Dictation**: 2 minutes from an *El hilo* or *Radio Ambulante* transcript, logged in `writing/dictations.md`.
- **Song**: write out last week's song from memory; then the next song on the Appendix J list: listen twice with the lyrics, look up every word, sing along 3 times. It is the daily song until the next song step.

**Episode pattern** (La casa de papel, Cien años de soledad): watch with Spanish subtitles (Weeks 2–18) or without (Week 19 on), no pausing. Then one 8-minute scene three times: with subtitles, without, and pausing to shadow twelve lines. Six sentences in Spanish on the plot and one on a term of law, police, banking or, for *Cien años*, a phrase of the novel's you recognised; 10 cards. Tracker note.

**Book pattern** (B2 form): audiobook chapter first where one exists; intensive on the first 30 pages of each book (at most 12 words per chapter to the notebook), extensive after that with at most 8 lookups per 30 pages; a 250-word review and a 4-minute oral discussion with the AI ("discute este libro conmigo; pregúntame por los personajes, el final y lo que me ha parecido") after each book; the Spanish Wikipedia entry on the author read *before* starting; and, after the review, the *Un libro una hora* episode on the book where one exists (Cadena SER's hour-long retelling of a classic with readings, free in every podcast app; it has *Nada*, *Crónica de una muerte anunciada*, *Pedro Páramo* and *El camino*), listened to in the commute slot, with two things it noticed that you did not written in the tracker.

**GRE pattern**: read the theme's explanation in *Uso de la gramática española: avanzado*, look up its terms in the RAE's *Nueva gramática básica* (so that you own the Spanish names: *oración subordinada, perífrasis, complemento de régimen, valor epistémico*), do the theme's exercises, check them against the key, then write 20 own sentences on the theme's points and run the Correction protocol. **BB pattern**: read the chapter, copy into the notebook every rule and example that was new to you, then write 20 sentences.

**SHADOW pattern**: one 3-minute segment a week from a *Radio Ambulante* or *El hilo* episode (both publish exact transcripts): listen twice, read, listen while reading, shadow five times, record it, compare with the original for rhythm, linking and intonation, mark the three places you were furthest off and repeat those ten times. Alternate Spain-born and Latin American voices where the episode offers them.

## Appendix A: What changes at B2

1. **No learner material.** Every source is made for native speakers except the two grammars and the DELE preparation book. When something is hard, the response is more of it, not something easier.
2. **Grammar twice.** First in Spanish with *Uso de la gramática española* and the RAE's *Nueva gramática básica*, so that you own the Spanish terms (*subordinada sustantiva, perífrasis, complemento de régimen, oración impersonal, valor de conjetura*) and can use the *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas* without translation; then the deep pass in Butt and Benjamin. After Week 30, grammar work is error analysis only.
3. **The rhythm carries the level.** Four *Hoy en EL PAÍS* a week, a long programme, two *Telediarios*, four voice sessions, a debate, a retelling, a dictation, a 300-word text, four articles, a song. That is 9 hours a week before any television or book. Do not trim it; trim the television if you must.
4. **Literature is the point.** The first Premio Nadal, García Márquez's tightest book, Spain's best-selling historical series, six of the most argued-over stories ever written, Argentina's leading living writer, the novel that made the Boom possible, the Castilian school classic, the most discussed Spanish novel of 2020, Neruda, and Cortázar. Every book is preceded by its author's Wikipedia entry and followed by a review and a discussion; that is how Spanish speakers talk about books, and you will be asked.
5. **Two spines on screen.** *La casa de papel* for Spanish crime, police and the speech of Madrid under pressure; *Cien años de soledad* for the Colombian Spanish of the novel you will read at C1. *Salvados* every week is the exam for both.
6. **Subtitles end in Week 18.** After that, subtitles are a checking tool, not a viewing aid.
7. **Speaking becomes argument.** Presentations of six minutes, debates against an assistant instructed to disagree, retelling the news aloud. The discourse markers (*bueno, o sea, es que, vamos, hombre, en fin, la verdad es que*) are now a requirement, counted at the exit.
8. **Writing becomes public.** The *carta al director* and the *artículo de opinión* are the genres of Spanish public debate. Your portfolio ends with a 500-word article you could send to a newspaper.
9. **Dictation from real journalism.** *El hilo* and *Radio Ambulante* publish exact transcripts of natural-speed speech from across Latin America. Your error rate should fall below 5 percent.
10. **Varieties appear.** You are not learning them; you are learning to hear them and to know which is which, and to keep your own consistent.

## Appendix B: B2 grammar checklist

1. **Word order in full**: postposed subjects for new information, fronting with clitic doubling, clitic placement with periphrases (*lo voy a hacer / voy a hacerlo*), heavy constituents moved to the end, cleft sentences of every shape.
2. **Cleft and emphasis**: *Fue X quien / el que ...*; *Lo que ... es ...*; *Es aquí donde ...*; *Por eso es por lo que ...* (never *es por eso que*); *sí que*; *lo* + adjective + *que* (*lo difícil que es*).
3. **Subordinate clause types** with the full subordinator set including *puesto que, dado que, de ahí que, con tal de que, siempre y cuando, a no ser que, salvo que, por mucho que, aun cuando, si bien, como si, ni que, de modo que, a medida que, conforme, según*.
4. **Tense and aspect**: *indefinido / imperfecto / perfecto / pluscuamperfecto* in narrative and reports; the *-ra* pluperfect of journalism; the historical present; the continuous forms and their limits; regional perfecto usage.
5. **Future, conditional and conjecture**: every epistemic use; the journalistic conditional; *deber de*; *a lo mejor / igual / lo mismo* with the indicative; *quizá / tal vez* with either mood.
6. **Subjunctive in noun clauses**: every trigger class, the meaning-switching verbs, *el hecho de que*, sequence of tenses, the perfect and pluperfect subjunctive.
7. **Subjunctive in adverbial and relative clauses**: time, purpose, concession, condition, manner, unknown and negated antecedents, *-quiera que* forms, set phrases.
8. **Past subjunctive and conditionals**: *-ra / -se*, all conditional types and the mixed type, *de* + infinitive, *como si / ni que*, *ojalá* in every tense, literary *-ra*, polite *quisiera / debiera / pudiera*.
9. **Reported speech**: complete backshift including subjunctive and imperative, reporting verbs with their regime, free indirect style, journalistic attribution verbs.
10. **Ser, estar, haber and the passives**: meaning-changing adjectives, event vs state participles, *ser* for events' place and time, *se* passive and its agreement, *se le*, the third-person plural, when Spanish avoids the passive.
11. **Every value of se**: reflexive, reciprocal, passive, impersonal, accidental, dative of interest, *se* for *le*, meaning-shifting pronominal verbs; *leísmo* norms by region.
12. **Relatives in depth**: *el cual* where required, *lo que / lo cual*, *cuyo*, restrictive vs explanatory and the comma, relatives with the subjunctive, the *que galicado* avoided.
13. **Verbal periphrases in full**: the gerund, infinitive and participle sets with their nuances (*ir, venir, andar, llevar, acabar, venir a, llegar a, dar por, tener, echarse a, romper a, quedar en, pasar a*).
14. **Prepositions and regime**: *por / para* in every use; *a / en / de / desde / hacia / hasta / bajo / tras / ante / contra / mediante / según*; 180 fixed combinations; *queísmo* and *dequeísmo* eliminated.
15. **Connectors and cohesion**: causal, adversative, additive, temporal, conclusive, reformulating; *de ahí que* + subjunctive; reference words (*dicho, lo anterior, al respecto, en este sentido, el primero / el segundo*).
16. **Nominal vs verbal style**: nominalisation and its support verbs (*llevar a cabo, poner en marcha, hacer hincapié, dar cuenta de, interponer, proceder a*); administrative Spanish unpacked into plain Spanish.
17. **Stance and opinion**: stance adverbs (*al parecer, supuestamente, presuntamente, sin duda, desde luego, difícilmente, lamentablemente*); evaluation with either mood; agreement and disagreement in register; concession in argument.
18. **Impersonal constructions**: *se* impersonal vs passive, *uno*, the third-person plural, *hay que / es preciso*, *haber* impersonal always singular, weather and time verbs.
19. **Punctuation in full**: the RAE 2010 rules; *solo* and demonstratives without accent; *guion*; prefixes; the colon, semicolon, dash, *raya*, parentheses and quotation marks in order; abbreviations.
20. **Spelling**: *b/v, g/j, ll/y, h*, *s/c/z* for *seseo* speakers, the accent system complete including *aún / aun, más / mas, sé / se*, capitals, numbers and units in text (*10 %, 3,5 millones, 1.500 €, siglo XXI*).
21. **Register**: formal letter and email conventions in full (*Estimado/a, Muy señor mío, A quien corresponda, Le escribo en relación con, Quedo a la espera, Atentamente, Un cordial saludo, Reciba un saludo*), workplace norms (*tú* in most Spanish workplaces, *usted* in Latin American ones and with strangers and elders), academic markers (*cabe señalar, en este sentido, a este respecto, conforme a*), spoken forms and swearing recognised (*joder, coño, hostia, la leche* in Spain; *carajo, chingar, boludo* in the Americas).
22. **Discourse markers, active**: *bueno, o sea, es que, vamos, hombre, mujer, en fin, la verdad es que, a ver, pues, venga, ¿sabes?, ¿no?, total que, y tal, y eso, ¿vale?* (Spain); *este, o sea, ¿viste?, dale, órale, ¿cierto?, pues sí* (Americas).
23. **Idioms and collocations**: 100 idioms (Appendix L of B1 plus fifty harvested); 100 verb-noun collocations (*tomar una decisión, plantear una pregunta, hacer frente a, tener en cuenta, dar por sentado, poner de manifiesto, sacar una conclusión, correr el riesgo, prestar atención, guardar silencio*).
24. **Comparison and quantity**: *el doble de ... que, la mitad de, tres veces más, cuanto más ... más, la mayoría de, un tercio de, de media, por ciento / puntos porcentuales, apenas, casi, más de, cerca de, en torno a*.
25. **Varieties**: main areas recognised by ear (Castilian north and south, Andalusian, Canarian, Mexican, Caribbean, Andean, Rioplatense, Chilean); *voseo* forms; *seseo, yeísmo rehilado, aspiration*; the written norm shared by all.

## Appendix E: B2 exit reading test

**La biblioteca es más que libros**

Cuando yo era niña, la biblioteca era el único sitio del pueblo donde se podía estar sin tener que comprar nada. Una podía pasarse una hora con un tebeo, llevarse un disco, preguntarle a la bibliotecaria por lo divino y lo humano y volver a casa con una bolsa de libros por la que nadie pedía dinero. Hoy, cuando la mayoría llevamos en el bolsillo el acceso a todo el saber del mundo, se ha puesto de moda preguntar si de verdad seguimos necesitando las bibliotecas públicas. La pregunta está mal planteada. Las necesitamos más que nunca, pero por razones distintas de las que solemos dar.

Es cierto que el préstamo de libros en papel ha bajado. Las cifras del INE muestran un descenso de alrededor de un tercio desde el cambio de siglo, y los políticos partidarios de recortar señalan esa curva cada vez que hay que negociar un presupuesto. Pero los datos de préstamo cuentan solo una parte pequeña de la historia. Al mismo tiempo, el número de visitantes ha subido en muchos municipios, porque las bibliotecas han asumido funciones nuevas: son lugares de encuentro, aulas de estudio, oficinas de atención al ciudadano y, en algunos pueblos, el único sitio con un aseo público y un enchufe que cualquiera puede usar.

La función más importante, sin embargo, sigue siendo la más difícil de medir. La biblioteca es uno de los últimos lugares donde personas de orígenes muy distintos comparten un espacio sin tener que hacer nada en concreto. El jubilado que lee el periódico, la recién llegada que practica español con una voluntaria, el estudiante de bachillerato que no puede concentrarse en casa y el hombre sin hogar que solo quiere sentarse al calor. Puede que no hablen entre ellos, pero se ven, y eso no es poca cosa en una sociedad en la que, cada vez más, solo tratamos con gente que se parece a nosotros.

Los críticos objetan que esto es romanticismo y que el dinero se podría gastar mejor en colegios o en residencias de mayores. Es un dilema real, porque los presupuestos municipales están asfixiados. Pero cuando se calcula lo que cuesta una biblioteca por habitante, más o menos lo que cuesta una entrada de cine al mes, cuesta encontrar otro servicio que dé tanto por tan poco. Y los ayuntamientos que han cerrado sucursales para ahorrar suelen descubrir que el ahorro era menor de lo previsto y la pérdida para el barrio, mayor.

Hay también un argumento lingüístico que rara vez se menciona. Para los miles de personas que cada año aprenden español de adultas, la biblioteca es la escuela de idiomas más barata y mejor que existe. Hay libros de lectura fácil, audiolibros, periódicos y, sobre todo, personas con las que se puede hablar sin pagar. Es difícil imaginar una aplicación que sustituya eso.

No pretendo que la biblioteca sea la de mi infancia. Tiene que evolucionar, y ya lo está haciendo. Pero debemos dejar de medirla por cuántos libros se prestan y empezar a medirla por lo que perdería el pueblo si no estuviera. La respuesta la conocemos casi todos. Solo hace falta recordarla la próxima vez que alguien llame a la biblioteca una reliquia del pasado.

*Inés Carballo, León*

Preguntas (responde en español, con frases completas):

- **A New Reference Grammar of Modern Spanish**, 6th ed.: the steps name chapters by topic (word order, tenses, subjunctive, ser/estar, passive, pronouns, articles, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, spelling, punctuation, forms of address); use its contents and index.
- **Shadowing texts**: *Radio Ambulante* (radioambulante.org; every episode has an exact Spanish transcript) and *El hilo* (elhilo.audio; weekly, transcripts on the site). Both are Latin American; for a Castilian voice, the *Documentos RNE* archive has no transcripts, so use the audiobooks of *Nada* or *El camino* for two of the SHADOW weeks if you want Spain-born rhythm.
- **Books** (all in print; Spanish audiobooks on Audible, Storytel or Apple Books where noted): Carmen Laforet, *Nada* (Destino, about 300 pages; audiobook exists); Gabriel García Márquez, *Crónica de una muerte anunciada* (Debolsillo, 128 pages; audiobook exists); Arturo Pérez-Reverte, *El capitán Alatriste* (Alfaguara, about 240 pages; audiobook exists); Jorge Luis Borges, *Ficciones* (Debolsillo; the six stories are also on Ciudad Seva); Mariana Enríquez, *Las cosas que perdimos en el fuego* (Anagrama, 208 pages, ISBN 9788433998064); Juan Rulfo, *Pedro Páramo* (Cátedra or Editorial RM, about 124 pages); Miguel Delibes, *El camino* (Destino, about 200 pages; audiobook exists); Sara Mesa, *Un amor* (Anagrama, 2020, about 190 pages); Pablo Neruda, *Veinte poemas de amor y una canción desesperada* (Wikisource, free); Elvira Sastre, *Baluarte* (Valparaíso, 2014) or *La soledad de un cuerpo acostumbrado a la herida* (Visor, 2016); Julio Cortázar's stories on Ciudad Seva. Read each author's Spanish Wikipedia entry before starting.
- **History reader**: Juan Eslava Galán, *Historia de España contada para escépticos* (Planeta; the 2024 edition has 656 pages; also as ebook and audiobook). Read the chapters from the Reyes Católicos to the Transición, about 300 pages. The alternative is García de Cortázar's *Breve historia de España* (Alianza), drier and fuller.
- **Memoria de España**: RTVE, 2004, 27 episodes of about 55 minutes, on YouTube in several playlists and on RTVE Play in Spain. The twelve used here, in order: *Los Reyes Católicos*; *Carlos V, un monarca, un imperio y una espada*; *Felipe II, un imperio sin emperador*; *La decadencia*; *Las Cortes de Cádiz y la Constitución de 1812* (with the War of Independence); *El reinado de Isabel II*; *La Restauración*; *La Segunda República*; *La Guerra Civil*; *Franco, Franco, Franco*; *La Transición*; *España en libertad*. Titles vary slightly between uploads; match by period.
- **Television**: *La casa de papel* (Netflix worldwide, Spanish audio and subtitles; parts 1–2 are 22 episodes), *Cien años de soledad* (Netflix, 2024–, 16 episodes; Colombian cast), *Valeria* (Netflix, 3 seasons; season 1 used), *Isabel* (RTVE, 2012–2014, 39 episodes; on Prime Video in many countries; season 1 used), *Salvados* (laSexta, 2008–; seasons on Prime Video and Netflix depending on country; a large archive on YouTube).

- **Podcasts**: *Hoy en EL PAÍS* (daily, about 20 minutes, free, in all apps), *El hilo* (weekly, transcripts), *Nadie sabe nada* (Cadena SER, Saturdays, about an hour; in all apps and on YouTube), *Documentos RNE* (RNE's documentary archive, hundreds of hour-long programmes, free on rtve.es/play/audios and in all apps), *Carne Cruda* (independent daily, carnecruda.es), *Radio Ambulante* (weekly), *La Script* (Cadena SER, film). *Un tema al día* (elDiario.es, daily, about 15 minutes) is an alternative to *Hoy en EL PAÍS* if you subscribe to elDiario.es instead.

- **Varieties**: the Instituto Cervantes' *Catálogo de voces hispánicas* (cvc.cervantes.es/lengua/voces_hispanicas), free video recordings with transcriptions from cities across Spain and the Americas; *Easy Spanish*'s city episodes; the *Diccionario de americanismos* (rae.es/damer) for Latin American words.
- **Frequency list for ranks 5001–8000**: the free open-subtitle word list for Spanish in the FrequencyWords project on GitHub (hermitdave/FrequencyWords, file `content/2018/es/es_50k.txt`); take ranks 5001–8000.
- **Dictionaries and usage**: dle.rae.es and rae.es/dpd; the free *Clave* dictionary at smdiccionarios.com (definitions written for learners and natives alike); María Moliner's *Diccionario de uso del español* (Gredos, in print and as an app) for the finest usage; *Diccionario de dichos y frases hechas* (Alberto Buitrago, Espasa, ISBN 9788467039412) for idioms; Fundéu (fundeu.es) for everything else.
- **DELE B2**: examenes.cervantes.es under DELE → Exámenes → B2 has the *modelo de examen* (four papers, audio, transcripts, keys) and the marking guides. *El Cronómetro B2* (Edinumen, edición Nuevo DELE, ISBN 9788498485486) has four complete models with keys and audio; *Preparación al DELE B2* (Edelsa) has seven. One of them is enough.
