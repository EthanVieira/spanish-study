# Level B1: Threshold

**Prerequisite:** Milestone A2 reached (step A2-331 ticked). The README methods still apply, and the A2 weekly patterns (podcast pattern, dictation, AI voice sessions, monologues, diario) continue with the changes below.

**Goal at exit:** you can follow the main points of clear standard Spanish on familiar topics (the news, a TV drama, a documentary, a conversation about work or opinions), handle almost any everyday situation including bureaucracy and the doctor, tell a story or explain an opinion in connected speech with reasons, read a children's novel, a young-adult novel, official information and newspaper articles, and write about 200 words of connected text in the right register (a formal letter to the town hall, an opinion piece, a personal narrative). Vocabulary about 4000 words. You know the complete grammar of Spanish, including the whole subjunctive system in its common uses, the three conditional types, reported speech, both passives, the verb + preposition combinations, and the connector system, and your pronunciation has natural linking, rhythm and intonation. This is the level of the DELE B1 and of the reading needed for the Spanish nationality test.

**Time:** the dailies take about 1 hour 45 minutes a day (half of it the commute slot and free reading, which need no desk) and the steps add up to about 480 hours: about 830 hours in all. At the planned pace, over 30 weeks, that is 2 hours 40 minutes of steps a day, 6 days a week; at one hour of steps a day the level takes about 80 weeks. Keep the order either way.

**What changes at B1** (Appendix A): the culture track is now in Spanish. *El ministerio del tiempo* is the television spine, one episode a week for 24 weeks, and it walks you through the Spanish canon in person. You read your first unabridged novels, six canon short stories and a Lorca play in the original, and the official nationality-test manual becomes your non-fiction reader. Grammar is a systematic pass through *Spanish: An Essential Grammar* from the first chapter to the last, with Butt and Benjamin beside it. News moves from graded articles to BBC Mundo, RTVE and, from Week 8, the *Telediario*. Beside the steps, two dailies carry the volume: the commute slot (*Hoy Hablamos* from Week 5 and the audiobook of the current book) and twenty minutes of free reading from a fixed list. Every week also has a *Cuéntame cómo pasó* step as a second series beside the casual one, and from Week 12 a *Radio Ambulante* step. Films go from seven to eleven.

## The B1 day

Every study day: **the dailies, in the order of the table, then the current step**, the first unticked step in this file. One step is one sitting with one focus; a step too long for today is resumed tomorrow. What used to be a weekly rhythm (podcasts, the news, the monologue, the dictation, the bank text, the song) and the weekly habits (the series, the nationality manual, the magazine) are now individual steps placed in order inside each week, so there is nothing to schedule and nothing running in the background except the dailies.

| Daily | Minutes | Starts at | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anki: all due cards, then 20 new (a third of them sentence cards) | 20 | A1-004 | never |
| Assimil second wave, one lesson | 10 | A2 | at lesson 100, about Week 7 |
| Commute slot: the *Duolingo Spanish Podcast*, then from Week 5 *Hoy Hablamos* and the audiobook of the current book | 30 | B1-009, B1-093 | B2 changes its content |
| AI voice conversation, topic fixed by the rule in B1-011 | 10 | B1-011 | never |
| The daily song, sung twice | 5 | A1 | never |
| Diario, 8 to 10 sentences, corrected on Sundays | 10 | B1-012 | end of B2 |
| Free reading, from the fixed list | 20 | B1-010 | never |

Dreaming Spanish stops being a daily at B1; its Intermediate and Advanced videos are good free-time viewing, but the series, the podcasts and the commute slot now carry the listening.

A week check passes when every step since the previous check is ticked and the dailies were done on at least six of the last seven days.

The steps that return every week are written as one line and use these patterns:

- **Podcast pattern** (ISP, ECJ, the first 5 minutes of *Radio Ambulante*): listen blind twice, read the transcript, listen with the text, write a 5-sentence summary and score it against the transcript, shadow 60 seconds, add 8 cards. Episodes are taken in order.
- **Telediario pattern**: watch the summary edition of the day on the RTVE Noticias YouTube channel without pausing; write the five headlines in Spanish; check them against rtve.es/noticias.
- **Monologue**: Recording protocol on the topic named, from Appendix F part 2, transcribed and corrected.
- **Dictation**: 2 minutes of audio from a source with a transcript, written in 5-second chunks, the error percentage logged in `writing/dictations.md`.
- **Song**: write out last week's song from memory and check it; then the new song from the Appendix J list, in order: listen twice with the lyrics, look up every word, sing along 3 times. It is the daily song until the next song step.

**Ministerio pattern** (Weeks 2–25): watch the episode once with Spanish subtitles, no pausing. Then choose one 8–10 minute scene, rewatch it three times: with subtitles, without, then pausing to shadow ten lines. Write six sentences in Spanish about what happened and one about the historical figure, period or work the episode visits. Add 10 cards. In the tracker note the episode number and that figure, period or work; when it is a writer or a book already in your canon list, link the two.

**Book pattern**: intensive first read (unknown words looked up, at most 15 written per chapter, 20–30 cards a week), audiobook chapter listened to *before* reading where an audiobook exists; extensive second read of each finished book straight through; then a 150-word review and a 3-minute oral retelling, both corrected.

**GRAM pattern**: read the chapter section named in the step, then the matching sections of BB (use its index), copy every rule you did not already know into the notebook with the book's example and one of your own, do the matching exercises in *Practising Spanish Grammar* and check them against its key, then write the number of sentences stated and run the Correction protocol.

- [ ] **B1-015** [Study · Deep · 60 min] Rhythm and linking, part 1.
      Do: Spanish is syllable-timed and runs words together. Rules: (1) a final consonant joins the next word's initial vowel (*los amigos* → *lo-sa-mi-gos*, *el hombre* → *e-lom-bre*); (2) two identical vowels across a word boundary merge into one (*la abuela* → *la-bue-la*, *está en* → *es-tán*); (3) two different vowels across a boundary form one syllable (*sinalefa*: *mi hermano* → *mier-ma-no*, *voy a ir* → *vo-yair*); (4) unstressed function words (*el, la, de, que, y, a, en, me, te, se*) have no stress of their own and lean on the next word. ## Appendix A: What changes at B1

1. **Spanish about Spanish.** Grammar help now comes first from the RAE's *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas* and Fundéu in Spanish, with GRAM as the systematic backbone in English and BB beside it. Culture reading is in Spanish (Spanish Wikipedia, the Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes, Ciudad Seva). If a page is too hard, use text-to-speech and read for gist; do not switch to English.
2. **Native speed is the norm.** *Hoy Hablamos*, the *Telediario*, *Radio Ambulante* and *El ministerio del tiempo* are made for native speakers. Extensive listening with no pausing is most of your listening time now; the podcast pattern (with transcripts) keeps the intensive side alive.
3. **The Ministerio as spine.** Twenty-four episodes, one a week. It is the series Spanish readers and teachers most love, its dialogue is clear studio Spanish, and every episode takes you to meet a figure from the canon or the history you are reading about: Lope, Cervantes, Velázquez, Lorca, Isabel la Católica, the Armada, the Civil War. The scene-shadowing part of the pattern is where the pronunciation gain is.
4. **Books, not readers.** Lindo, Paz, Jiménez, Sierra i Fabra, Lorca, Sender, and six of the most anthologised stories in the language. The book pattern's intensive-then-extensive rule matters more the longer the book: with *Campos de fresas* you go extensive after 40 pages.
5. **Non-fiction is official.** The CCSE manual is written in plain Spanish about the things Spaniards assume everyone knows. Read all of it and drill its questions.
6. **The steps that return every week** hold the A2 habits at higher volume: two podcasts, the news twice, a monologue, a dictation, a bank text, three articles, a song. They are not optional; with the dailies they are most of the hours.
7. **Grammar goes systematic.** Every chapter of GRAM in order, with BB's sections and the *Practising Spanish Grammar* exercises, and your own sentences as the test. The subjunctive gets three full weeks because it is the difference between B1 and everything below. By Week 23 nothing in the grammar should be new; from then on grammar work is only repair.
8. **Pronunciation moves to rhythm.** Linking, syllable timing, intonation and unstressed function words are what make you sound Spanish rather than understandable. The two rhythm steps and scene shadowing carry this.
9. **The DELE B1 is the model exam.** Its reading, listening, writing and oral formats shape the last five weeks; the official *modelo* is the exit check, and the CCSE practice test sits beside it.
10. **Time.** The level is long. Count weeks completed, not weeks elapsed: a missed week is added at the end, not skipped.

## Appendix B: B1 grammar checklist

1. **Nouns**: gender in full including double-gender and person nouns, plurals of compounds and foreign words, Spanish plural-only and singular-only nouns, collectives.
2. **Articles**: the definite article with generic, abstract and body-part nouns; omission with professions and languages; *el* before stressed *a-*; neuter *lo*; *un* omitted after *ser, sin, con, tener*; never *un otro*.
3. **Adjectives**: agreement, apocopation, position and meaning change (*viejo, gran, pobre, mismo, cierto, nuevo, único, antiguo*), adjectives as nouns, full comparison including *cuanto más ... más*, *cada vez más*, *-ísimo*.
4. **Tenses**: all nine indicative tenses, progressive forms, future and conditional of probability, *ir a* vs future, the Latin American perfecto/indefinido preference.
5. ***Ser, estar, haber***: meaning-changing adjectives, *estar* + participle vs *ser* + participle, *estar de*, *estar por/para*, *hay* in all tenses.
6. **Subjunctive, subordinate clauses**: after wishes, emotion, doubt, denial, influence, impersonal judgments; indicative with affirmative belief; same-subject infinitive.
7. **Subjunctive, adverbial and relative clauses**: purpose, future time, condition, concession (*aunque* either mood), manner, unknown antecedent, *quizás/tal vez*, set phrases (*sea lo que sea, pase lo que pase, que yo sepa*).
8. **Past subjunctive and conditionals**: *-ra/-se* forms, sequence of tenses, the three conditional types and the mixed type, *como si*, unreal *ojalá*, *de haber* + participle.
9. **Reported speech**: tense and word shifts, questions, commands with the subjunctive, unshifted truths.
10. **Irregular verbs**: the A2 sixty plus: *caber, valer, satisfacer, adquirir, argüir, bendecir, maldecir, conducir, deducir, producir, reducir, traducir, lucir, nacer, crecer, merecer, obedecer, ofrecer, parecer, pertenecer, agradecer, aparecer, establecer, reconocer, competir, corregir, despedir, elegir, freír, impedir, medir, perseguir, reñir, sonreír, teñir, advertir, consentir, convertir, herir, hervir, mentir, preferir, sugerir, adormecer, atraer, distraer, contraer, oler, jugar, avergonzar, forzar, rogar, colgar, contar, mostrar, probar, recordar, soñar, volar, morder, mover* (checked on SpanishDict; regular ones dropped).
11. **Object pronouns in full**: *le → se*, order, redundancy, *leísmo* recognised, accidental *se*, prepositional forms, *conmigo/contigo/consigo*.
12. **Passives and *se***: *ser* + participle with *por*, *se* passive with agreement, impersonal *se*, third-person plural, *estar* + participle for states.
13. **Pronominal verbs with meaning shift**: *ir/irse, dormir/dormirse, quedar/quedarse, llevar/llevarse, comer/comerse, acordar/acordarse, parecer/parecerse, poner/ponerse, volver/volverse, hacer/hacerse*.
14. **Word order and emphasis**: postposed subjects for new information, fronted objects with a repeated pronoun, cleft sentences (*Fue ... quien / Lo que ... es / Es ... donde*).
15. **Relatives in full**: *que, el que, el cual, quien, cuyo, donde, cuando, como, lo que, lo cual*; the subjunctive with unknown antecedents; the preposition before the relative.
16. **Verbal periphrases**: *estar/seguir/ir/venir/andar/llevar* + gerund; *acabar de, volver a, dejar de, empezar a, ponerse a, echarse a, estar a punto de, tener que, deber, deber de, haber de, haber que, soler, ir a*; *tener/llevar* + participle.
17. **Verb, adjective and noun + preposition**: sixty combinations (*acordarse de, alegrarse de, arrepentirse de, darse cuenta de, dejar de, depender de, enamorarse de, olvidarse de, quejarse de, tratar de, acostumbrarse a, aprender a, ayudar a, atreverse a, dedicarse a, empezar a, enseñar a, invitar a, negarse a, parecerse a, volver a, casarse con, contar con, soñar con, quedar con, confiar en, consistir en, fijarse en, insistir en, pensar en, tardar en, preocuparse por, interesarse por, luchar por, optar por, estar acostumbrado a, estar dispuesto a, ser aficionado a, estar cansado de, estar harto de, estar seguro de, estar orgulloso de, estar enamorado de, estar interesado en, ser bueno en, estar de acuerdo con, estar contento con, ser capaz de, tener ganas de, tener miedo de, tener cuidado con, tener razón en, el interés por, la razón de, la respuesta a, la falta de, la necesidad de, el derecho a, la esperanza de, el miedo a*).
18. **Comparison advanced**: *cuanto más ... más, cada vez más, tanto ... como, igual de ... que, el doble de, más de lo que, no ... sino, no solo ... sino también*.
19. **Coordination and correlatives**: *tanto ... como, ni ... ni, o ... o, o bien ... o bien, no ... sino, no solo ... sino (también), ya ... ya, bien ... bien*.
20. **Word formation**: nominalising, adjectival and verbal suffixes; prefixes; appreciative suffixes and their regional flavour; compounds.
21. **Orthography**: the full accent system including hiatus, *tilde diacrítica*, *-mente* adverbs and enclitics; *b/v*, *g/j*, *ll/y*, *h* traps; *porque / por qué / porqué / por que*; *sino / si no*; *a ver / haber*; *hay / ahí / ay*; capitals only for names.
22. **Punctuation**: comma rules (never between subject and verb, before *pero*, after a fronted clause, not before *y* in lists), opening *¿* and *¡* where the question begins, dialogue dashes, *«»*, the colon in letters.
23. **Discourse markers in use**: *pues, bueno, vale, o sea, es que, hombre, venga, anda, a ver, claro, mira, oye, en fin, total que, vamos, ya, ¿sabes?, ¿no?*.
24. **Register**: formal letter conventions (*Estimado/a señor/a:, Muy señores míos:, Le escribo para, Quedo a la espera de, Atentamente, Un cordial saludo* vs *Hola, Querida, Un abrazo, Besos*), *usted* and its pronouns and possessives, *vos* recognised, spoken reductions (*pa'*, *na'*, *to'*) recognised, casual intensifiers (*mogollón, un montón, flipar, molar, chido*) recognised.
25. **Connectors**: *en primer lugar, además, asimismo, por otro lado, en cambio, sin embargo, no obstante, por lo tanto, por consiguiente, es decir, o sea, por ejemplo, en resumen, en definitiva, de hecho, en realidad, de todos modos, al fin y al cabo, a pesar de (que), aunque, ya que, puesto que, así que, de modo que*.

El Ayuntamiento aceptó hacer una prueba de tres meses, y ahora el pleno municipal tiene que decidir si la medida continúa.

Yo creo que la prueba ha sido un éxito. El primer domingo, al pasear por la calle Santiago, de repente había sitio para todo lo que normalmente queda arrinconado: niños dibujando con tizas en el asfalto, ancianos sentados en los bancos charlando, y una cafetería que había sacado mesas donde suelen aparcar los coches. El ruido había desaparecido. Se oían los pájaros.

Por supuesto, también hay inconvenientes. Los comerciantes del centro están divididos. Algunos cuentan que han tenido más clientes de lo habitual, porque la gente se queda más tiempo cuando no tiene que estar pendiente del parquímetro. Otros dicen que los clientes de los pueblos de alrededor han dejado de venir, porque no les apetece aparcar fuera del centro y andar el último tramo. Por eso la asociación de comerciantes ha propuesto un compromiso: un domingo sin coches al mes en lugar de cada semana.

Entiendo la preocupación, pero creo que es exagerada. En otras ciudades que han probado lo mismo, las ventas bajaron un poco las primeras semanas y luego volvieron a subir. La gente se acostumbra. También costó que los españoles se acostumbraran a la prohibición de fumar en los bares, y hoy nadie quiere volver atrás.

Sin embargo, el argumento más importante no es el comercio, sino la salud y la seguridad. Cada año hay niños heridos en accidentes de tráfico en las ciudades españolas, y la mayoría de los accidentes ocurren en trayectos cortos, cerca de casa. Si podemos darles a los niños aunque sea un día a la semana en el que puedan moverse con libertad, habremos hecho algo que merece la pena.

Por eso espero que el pleno vote a favor de mantener la medida, y que los demás municipios de Castilla y León se animen a imitarla. No cuesta casi nada, no hace falta construir nada nuevo, y nos deja una ciudad en la que de verdad apetece estar.

*Marta Cristóbal, Valladolid*
