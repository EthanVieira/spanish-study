# Spanish Self-Study Roadmap: Zero to C2

A literal, sequential, checkbox-driven guide for learning Spanish entirely by self-study, with no tutor and no human speaking partner. Follow the steps in order. Each step has a **Done when** line that tells you exactly what "finished" means. Do not skip ahead.

## How to read a step

Every step looks like this:

```
- [ ] A1-040 [Study · Focused · 45 min] Complete Spanish, Unit 3: listen and read.
      Do: ...
      Done when: ...
```

- **ID** (A1-040): level and sequence number. Steps are prerequisites for everything after them.
- **Type tag**:
  - `Setup`: installing, buying, registering, organising.
  - `Study`: working through a lesson with a textbook or course.
  - `Drill`: memorisation and repetition (Anki, shadowing, copying).
  - `Input`: listening, reading, watching for understanding.
  - `Produce`: writing or speaking (recorded) output.
  - `Culture`: orientation in the literature, film, TV and societies of the Spanish-speaking world, often in English at low levels.
  - `Check`: a verification step with a pass threshold. If you fail, the step tells you which earlier steps to repeat.
  - `Habit`: a recurring routine that starts at this step and continues until the guide says otherwise.
- **Effort tag**:
  - `Casual`: background or relaxed activity. Dictionary lookups optional. You can be tired.
  - `Focused`: normal study. Sit at a desk, no phone.
  - `Deep`: intensive study. Everything gets looked up, written down, and reviewed. Plan for the full time.
- **Time**: a realistic estimate for one sitting. A step may be split over several days unless it says "one sitting".

## The five standard methods

These are referred to by name throughout the guide so they are defined once here.

### Method 1: LRS cycle (Listen, Read, Shadow)

Used for every textbook dialogue, podcast segment or short audio clip.

1. **Listen blind**: play the audio 3 times without any text. After the third time, write down in English what you think happened and any Spanish words you caught.
2. **Read**: read the transcript or dialogue slowly. Look up every unknown word on SpanishDict (spanishdict.com) or in the textbook glossary. Write unknown words in your notebook with the sentence they appeared in.
3. **Listen with text**: play the audio while reading along, 2 times.
4. **Shadow**: play the audio one sentence at a time. Pause, repeat the sentence aloud imitating rhythm, vowel purity, stress and linking as exactly as you can. Do the whole dialogue this way twice.
5. **Shadow live**: play the audio and speak along with it simultaneously, without pausing, 3 times.
6. **Record**: record yourself reading the dialogue aloud without the audio. Listen back once. Note the 3 words you were least happy with and look them up on SpanishDict or Forvo to hear a native pronunciation. Say each 10 times.

### Method 2: Anki card rules

- One deck named `Español`. Sub-decks are not needed.
- Card format: front = the Spanish word **in a short phrase or sentence** (never the bare word alone), back = English meaning plus gender for nouns (`el`/`la`) and the plural, plus the irregular forms for verbs once you have learned them (yo-form, stem change, participle).
  - Good front: `Vivo en **Madrid**.` Back: `Madrid (proper noun)`.
  - Good front: `la **ciudad**` Back: `city · las ciudades (f.)`.
  - Good front: `Yo **tengo** dos hermanos.` Back: `to have · tener (tengo, tienes, tiene ...) · participle tenido`.
  - Bad front: `ciudad`.
- Add audio when a resource provides it. For textbook words, use SpanishDict or Forvo audio. Do not skip words because they lack audio.
- Write every accent mark. A card with `esta` when you mean `está` is a wrong card. On a phone, hold the vowel key to get the accented letter; on a computer, learn the shortcuts on day one (step A1-002).
- Settings: 15 new cards per day, 200 max reviews per day, default FSRS scheduling. Never "reset" the deck.
- **Anki is a daily, non-negotiable habit** from step A1-004 onward through C2. Missing days is the single biggest cause of failure in self-study.
- "Mature" below means a card with an interval of 21 days or more (visible in Anki Stats).

### Method 3: Recording protocol

Used for all `Produce` speaking steps.

1. Read the prompt. You get 2 minutes of silent planning. You may write 5 keywords, no more.
2. Record with your phone's voice recorder. Do not stop or restart. Silence is allowed, quitting is not.
3. Name the file `YYYY-MM-DD-<stepID>.m4a` and keep it in a `recordings` folder. You will listen to old recordings at milestones to hear your progress.
4. Listen back once. Write down every error you notice yourself. Then transcribe what you said (as accurately as you can, errors included) and run it through the correction protocol below.

### Method 4: Correction protocol (AI-assisted)

Because there is no tutor, an AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT or similar) is your correction service. Save this prompt and reuse it every time:

```
You are a strict but kind Spanish teacher. I am a [A1/A2/...] learner. Below is my Spanish text.
1. List every error as a table: my version | corrected version | one-line rule.
2. Give a fully corrected version.
3. Give a version a native speaker at my level would naturally write, keeping my meaning.
4. Tell me the 2 grammar points I should review.
Treat a missing or wrong accent mark as an error. Do not add content I did not write.
```

Rules: read every correction and copy each corrected sentence into your notebook by hand. If the same error type appears 3 times across texts, add a card to Anki with the rule and an example.

### Method 5: Intensive vs. extensive input

- **Intensive** (`Deep` tag): short text or clip. Understand 100 percent. Every unknown word looked up and written down. Reread or replay until it is fully clear.
- **Extensive** (`Casual` tag): long text or clip. Tolerate not understanding. No pausing, no dictionary unless a word appears 3 or more times. The goal is volume and getting used to real speed.

Both are required. Intensive builds the system; extensive makes it automatic.

## Daily routine

The guide assumes roughly **90 to 100 minutes a day, 6 days a week**, with one longer session at the weekend. If you have less time, keep the order of steps and stretch the calendar; do not drop steps. A suggested daily shape at A1:

| Slot | Minutes | What |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | 15 | Anki reviews (all due cards, then the new ones) |
| Commute or chores | 25 | Language Transfer tracks, later shadowing playback |
| Evening | 30–45 | The next `Study` or `Drill` step in the guide |
| Before bed | 15 | A Dreaming Spanish video, a song, or a page of Gloria Fuertes |

## Rules that never change

1. **Order is law.** Do steps in sequence. A step assumes every previous step is done.
2. **Done when means done when.** Do not tick a step whose criterion you have not met.
3. **Fail a Check, go back.** Every `Check` step lists what to repeat. Repeat it, then retake the check. There is no penalty; there is also no bypass.
4. **Anki every day.** See Method 2.
5. **Speak out loud every day**, even at A1, even alone. Spanish pronunciation is not hard, but the trilled *rr*, pure vowels and natural linking only come from daily use of the mouth.
6. **Keep a paper notebook.** Grammar notes, corrected sentences, verb tables and word lists go in it by hand. Handwriting slows you down in the useful way, and it forces you to write the accent marks.
7. **Track the calendar, not the clock.** Log the date you finish each step in the margin. Milestone checks ask you to compare old and new recordings and texts.

## A note on Spanish specifically

- **Grammar is the problem, not sound.** Spanish spelling is nearly phonetic and the sound system is small: five pure vowels and a handful of consonants that differ from English (*rr*, *r*, *j*, *ñ*, the soft *d* and *b/v* between vowels). What takes years is the verb: six persons, a dozen tenses, two past tenses that English merges, and the subjunctive from B1 on. The guide front-loads verb tables and never lets them drop.
- **Ser and estar** both mean "to be" and are chosen by meaning, not by convenience. *Estoy aburrido* (I'm bored) and *soy aburrido* (I'm boring) is the difference. There is a dedicated step for this at A1 and it is retested at every level.
- **Speech is fast.** Spanish has one of the highest syllable rates of any major language, and words run together (*¿Cómo estás?* sounds like *comoestás*). Learner audio is slow; real speech is not. The children's television, Dreaming Spanish videos and films in this guide are there to bridge the gap.
- **Two varieties, one language.** The guide uses the Spanish of Spain (Castilian) as the pronunciation model and grammar default because most of the free Spanish-made resources (Instituto Cervantes, RTVE, Difusión) come from Spain and the DELE exam is set there. If you have a reason to prefer Latin American Spanish (family, work, where you live), say so at step A1-009 and follow the *Latin American option* lines wherever they appear; the differences at A1 are *vosotros*, the *c/z* sound, and which past tense you reach for first. The literature and film canon in this guide is deliberately drawn from both continents.
- **Literature and screen culture** are a stated goal of this programme. Every level includes a `Culture` track that builds familiarity with the canon in Spanish (Cervantes, Lorca, Borges, García Márquez, Neruda, Cortázar, Rulfo, and the living generation) and with the film and television that Spanish speakers actually reference (Almodóvar, Buñuel, *Cuéntame*, *La casa de papel*, *El laberinto del fauno*, *Roma*, *El secreto de sus ojos*). At A1 and A2 much of this is done in English so that, by B1, you already know what you are reading.
